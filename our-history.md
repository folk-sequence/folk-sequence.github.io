I sat down with a guitar and a hot mic and didn't get up until there was a song. That's the whole method, if you can even call it that. No plan, no demo, no scratch track to beat — just a rhythm part looping in Bitwig while I figured out what I was singing about.

Somewhere around the third or fourth pass, the words showed up. We will learn from our friendship. And then the strange one I didn't expect, the one that made me want to keep going: there's a monk somewhere in a deep dark cave, writing down all the things we should save.

I don't totally know what that line means yet. I think it's about memory. About how the small stuff — a friendship, a morning, a take you almost threw away — is the stuff worth keeping. So I kept the take.

The video is the take. The whole thing, start to finish, no edits. You watch me loop the rhythm guitar, test the vocal mic about forty times, sing the same line until it sits right, and then layer the lead on top. It isn't a tutorial. It's just what it actually looks like to make one of these, dead air and all, until the dead air turns into a song.

Every guitar sound in this runs through plug-ins I build at Jalopy Music. The amp is Tackle Box — I had it on the "Boutique Glass" preset for the rhythm part and "Chime '60s" for the lead. In front of it and around it is a Manifold pedalboard. On the rhythm side that's Backroad Overdrive into a Console Preamp, a Tide Vibe, a Pressbox Compressor, a Twin Span Delay, and a Skyway Reverb. On the lead side I swapped in a Rotunda Rotary, a Treadle Wah, a Citrus Compressor, a Boxcar Octaver, and more Backroad.

None of it is a magic box. It's the same knowledge you'd get from a good amp and a pedal you actually understand — just in software, one-time purchase, no subscription, no DRM. If the song makes you want the sound, it's all at jalopy.music. And if you just want to hear the finished thing, Our History is up on songs.jalopy.music with the rest of Precious Jim Stone.

— Brian M Edwards (Precious Jim Stone · Jalopy Music)
